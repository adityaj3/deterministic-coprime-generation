### Overview

This repository provides a header-only, highly optimized C++20 engine for the deterministic generation of arbitrarily large pairwise coprime integer sequences. Classical sequence generation relies heavily on probabilistic rejection sampling, which introduces unpredictable execution latency due to Jacobsthal coprime deserts. Conversely, primorial arithmetic progressions bypass probability but induce factorial bit-length explosion.

This engine resolves both bottlenecks. By combining $S_m$-regular seed purification with non-linear algebraic mappings, specifically Generalized Cyclotomic Polynomials $\Phi_\ell(x)$ and Strong Divisibility Sequences (SDS), we achieve strict $\mathcal{O}(1)$ deterministic pairwise coprimality. This framework guarantees absolute mathematical isolation of prime factors while enforcing an exact asymptotic spatial memory contraction of $51.8789\%$ against primorial baselines.

## PART II: FORMAL MATHEMATICAL PROOFS & INVARIANTS

### Section 1: Axiomatic Framework

To eliminate the inherent factor dependencies of arbitrary multiprecision seed inputs, we extract a strictly sanitized arithmetic basis.

**Definition ($S_m$-Regular Element):** Given an arbitrary integer $N \in \mathbb{N}$ and a bounding prime set $S_m = \{2, 3, \dots, p_m\}$, the sanitized $S_m$-regular seed is algebraically defined as:

$$A_{\text{seed}} = \max\left(2, \frac{N}{\prod_{p_k \in S_m} p_k^{v_{p_k}(N)}}\right)$$

By construction, $A_{\text{seed}}$ is entirely stripped of all prime factors present in $S_m$. The boundary $\max(2, \dots)$ rigidly prevents algorithmic degeneration to trivial unit states ($A \in \{0, 1\}$).

### Section 2: Track 1 Generalized Cyclotomic Mapping Proof

**Theorem:** Let $A \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 2}$ be an $S_m$-regular seed. Let $\mathcal{P} = \{\ell_1, \ell_2, \dots, \ell_M\}$ be a set of distinct odd primes ($\ell_i \ge 3$). The sequence generated by the cyclotomic mapping:

$$d_i = \Phi_{\ell_i}(A) = \frac{A^{\ell_i} - 1}{A - 1} = \sum_{k=0}^{\ell_i-1} A^k$$

is strictly pairwise coprime.

**Proof (by Multiplicative Order $\mathrm{ord}_q(A)$):**

Assume for the sake of contradiction there exists a prime $q$ that divides both $d_i = \Phi_{\ell_i}(A)$ and $d_j = \Phi_{\ell_j}(A)$ for distinct evaluation primes $\ell_i \neq \ell_j$. Since $q \mid \frac{A^{\ell_i} - 1}{A - 1}$, it follows that $q \mid (A^{\ell_i} - 1)$ and $q \mid (A^{\ell_j} - 1)$. This congruence dictates that the multiplicative order of $A$ modulo $q$ must satisfy:

$$\mathrm{ord}_q(A) \mid \ell_i \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{ord}_q(A) \mid \ell_j$$

Since $\ell_i$ and $\ell_j$ are distinct prime numbers, $\gcd(\ell_i, \ell_j) = 1$. Therefore, $\mathrm{ord}_q(A) = 1$. The condition $\mathrm{ord}_q(A) = 1$ implies $A \equiv 1 \pmod q$, and consequently $q \mid (A-1)$. Substituting $A \equiv 1 \pmod q$ into the cyclotomic polynomial expansion yields:

$$\Phi_{\ell_i}(A) = \sum_{k=0}^{\ell_i-1} A^k \equiv \sum_{k=0}^{\ell_i-1} 1^k \equiv \ell_i \pmod q$$

Since $q \mid \Phi_{\ell_i}(A)$, we must have $q = \ell_i$. By symmetric reasoning, $\Phi_{\ell_j}(A) \equiv \ell_j \pmod q$, enforcing $q = \ell_j$. This implies $\ell_i = \ell_j$, contradicting the premise that $\ell_i$ and $\ell_j$ are distinct primes. Thus, no common prime divisor $q$ exists, and $\gcd(\Phi_{\ell_i}(A), \Phi_{\ell_j}(A)) = 1$.

**Corroboration via Lehmer's Resultant Identity:**

By Emma T. Lehmer's theorem on the resultant of two cyclotomic polynomials, for any distinct primes $\ell_i, \ell_j$:

$$\mathrm{Res}(\Phi_{\ell_i}(x), \Phi_{\ell_j}(x)) = 1$$

Because $\Phi_{\ell_i}(x)$ and $\Phi_{\ell_j}(x)$ are monic polynomials in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, Bezout's identity guarantees polynomials $U(x), V(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ such that $U(x)\Phi_{\ell_i}(x) + V(x)\Phi_{\ell_j}(x) = 1$. Evaluating this at $x = A$ yields strictly $1 \in \mathbb{Z}$.

### Section 3: Track 2 Strong Divisibility Sequence Proof

Evaluating a Strong Divisibility Sequence (SDS), such as the Fibonacci sequence $F_n$, at distinct prime indices $\ell_i \ge 2$ autonomously generates a pairwise coprime set since $\gcd(F_{\ell_i}, F_{\ell_j}) = F_{\gcd(\ell_i, \ell_j)} = F_1 = 1$.

**Theorem (Asymptotic Spatial Contraction):** The SDS framework yields an exact asymptotic spatial memory reduction of $1 - \ln(\phi) \approx 51.8789\%$ compared to linear primorial algorithms.

**Proof:**

By the Prime Number Theorem and Chebyshev's first function $\vartheta(x) \sim x$, the asymptotic bit-length of the $M$-th element in a primorial sequence $P_M\\# = \prod_{k=1}^M \ell_k$ is bounded by:

$$L_{\text{prim}}(M) = \log_2(P_M\\#) = \log_2(e) \cdot \vartheta(\ell_M) \sim \ell_M \log_2(e)$$

Conversely, the continuous evaluation of the $M$-th element in the SDS track is governed by Binet's formula $F_n = \frac{\phi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt{5}}$. As $n \to \infty$, the bit-length bounds tightly to:

$$L_{\text{sds}}(M) = \log_2(F_{\ell_M}) \sim \ell_M \log_2(\phi) - \frac{1}{2}\log_2(5)$$

Taking the ratio of their spatial footprints as $M \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{M \to \infty} \frac{L_{\text{sds}}(M)}{L_{\text{prim}}(M)} = \frac{\ell_M \log_2(\phi)}{\ell_M \log_2(e)} = \frac{\ln(\phi)}{\ln(e)} = \ln(\phi) \approx 0.4812118$$

Subtracting from unity yields the geometric memory contraction: $1 - \ln(\phi) \approx 0.5187882 \quad (51.8789\%)$.

### Section 4: Dedekind Domain Extension & Principal Ideal Norm Preservation

When extending the integer mappings to the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ of an algebraic number field $K/\mathbb{Q}$, we must rigorously bypass the "Split-Prime Norm Fallacy," which posits that the absolute field norm $\mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}$ does not preserve coprimality for arbitrary ideals due to the collapsing of completely split primes.

**Theorem (Unconditional Coprimality Preservation under Absolute Norm):** Let $\mathfrak{q}_i = \langle d_i \rangle = d_i \mathcal{O}_K$ and $\mathfrak{q}_j = \langle d_j \rangle = d_j \mathcal{O}_K$ be principal ideals generated by natively derived rational integers $d_i, d_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ from the established sequences. The absolute field norm unconditionally preserves coprimality:

$$\gcd(\mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}_i), \mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}_j)) = 1 \quad \text{in} \quad \mathbb{Z}$$

**Proof:**

The absolute field norm of a principal ideal generated by a rational integer $d \in \mathbb{Z}$ in a field extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $n = [K : \mathbb{Q}]$ evaluates strictly to scalar exponentiation:

$$\mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(d \mathcal{O}_K) = \vert{}d\vert{}^n$$

Since $\gcd(d_i, d_j) = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ (as proven in Section 2 and 3), the fundamental theorem of arithmetic guarantees that exponentiating these integers by $n$ introduces no new prime factors into their respective factorizations. Thus:

$$\gcd(\vert{}d_i\vert{}^n, \vert{}d_j\vert{}^n) = 1$$

Although the rational primes dividing $d_i$ may split completely in $\mathcal{O}_K$, the principal ideal $\mathfrak{q}_i = d_i \mathcal{O}_K$ inherently recombines all conjugate split prime ideals. Consequently, the absolute norm projection acts strictly as an exponentiation mapping over $\mathbb{Z}$, successfully insulating the framework from the Split-Prime Norm Fallacy.
